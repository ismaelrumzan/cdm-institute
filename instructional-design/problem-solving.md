Based on my research, here are several evidence-based frameworks for teaching problem-solving to coders and software engineers, particularly web developers, that have been validated through mixed-method research approaches:

## Computational Thinking Framework

A recent 2024 study evaluated computational thinking skills among Engineering and Computer Science students using a multi-method approach by combining quantitative methods (CTT scores and CTS responses) with qualitative methods (thematic analysis of open-ended questions), integrating objective assessments, self-perception scales, and qualitative insights. This framework breaks down problem-solving into core components:

**Step-by-Step Approach:**

1. **Decomposition** - Breaking complex problems into smaller, manageable parts
2. **Pattern Recognition** - Identifying similarities and trends in data/problems
3. **Abstraction** - Focusing on essential features while ignoring irrelevant details
4. **Algorithm Design** - Creating step-by-step solutions
5. **Debugging** - Systematic error identification and correction

Research shows that computational thinking refers to learners' ability to approach ill-structured tasks systematically based on algorithmic thinking in computing, and researchers have emphasized the role of teaching programming in facilitating learners' problem-solving skills.

## Problem-Based Learning (PBL) Framework

Research investigating the use of Problem Based Learning (PBL) and its application to software engineering found that through a sound pedagogical approach, the key skills of PBL (as endorsed by PBL practitioners, such as problem solving and independent learning) can be developed.

**Step-by-Step PBL Process:**

1. **Problem Presentation** - Real-world scenarios introduced
2. **Problem Analysis** - Students identify what they know and need to learn
3. **Learning Issues Generation** - Specific learning objectives defined
4. **Self-Directed Learning** - Independent research and skill acquisition
5. **Knowledge Application** - Applying learned concepts to solve the problem
6. **Reflection and Assessment** - Evaluating both process and outcomes

## Expert Problem-Solving Framework for STEM/Engineering

A comprehensive study interviewing 52 successful scientists and engineers developed a detailed characterization of the science and engineering problem-solving process as a set of specific interlinked decisions, with this framework being empirically grounded and describing the entire process.

**The Expert Decision Framework includes:**

1. **Problem Definition** - Understanding and scoping the problem
2. **Solution Strategy Selection** - Choosing appropriate approaches
3. **Implementation Planning** - Breaking down solution into actionable steps
4. **Execution and Monitoring** - Implementing while tracking progress
5. **Evaluation and Iteration** - Assessing results and refining approaches

## STEM Problem-Solving Framework

Recent developments in STEM and computer science education put a strong emphasis on twenty-first-century skills, such as solving authentic problems, with skills that typically transcend single disciplines. This interdisciplinary approach has been validated through research combining both qualitative and quantitative measures.

## Mixed-Method Assessment Approaches

The research consistently shows that effective evaluation of these frameworks requires both quantitative and qualitative measures:

- **Quantitative measures**: Performance scores, completion rates, error analysis, time-to-solution metrics
- **Qualitative measures**: Think-aloud protocols, reflective journals, peer assessments, portfolio evaluations, interview data

A 2025 comprehensive review found that teaching coding and computational thinking is now an emerging educational imperative, embedded in compulsory curriculum in the United States, Finland, the UK, and other countries, indicating the growing research base and practical implementation of these frameworks.

## Practical Implementation for Web Developers

For web development specifically, these frameworks translate into:

1. **Project-Based Learning** with real client problems
2. **Code Review Processes** that emphasize problem-solving reasoning
3. **Debugging Workshops** using systematic approaches
4. **Portfolio Development** with reflection components
5. **Peer Programming** sessions focused on problem decomposition

The research indicates that the most effective approaches combine structured, step-by-step frameworks with authentic, complex problems that mirror real-world software development challenges, assessed through both objective performance measures and subjective reflection/analysis methods.
